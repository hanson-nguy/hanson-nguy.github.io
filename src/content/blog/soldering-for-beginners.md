# Soldering for Beginners: Tools, Technique, and Mistakes

## The minimal tool list

You do not need a fancy station to start. This is enough to build almost any kit:

- A **25–40W iron** with a conical or chisel tip (temperature-controlled is nice, not required).
- **0.8mm rosin-core solder** (63/37 tin-lead flows better than lead-free for learners).
- A damp sponge or brass wool to clean the tip.
- Helping hands or a third-hand tool for holding the board.

Everything else — fume extractor, flux pen, finer tips — is an upgrade, not a requirement.

## Technique that matters

The secret is heating the joint, not the solder:

1. Tin the tip before every joint.
2. Touch the tip to the **pad and lead together** for a beat.
3. Feed solder into the joint — not onto the iron.
4. Pull away solder, then iron, and let it cool still.

A good joint looks like a shiny volcano. A cold joint is dull and grainy — it will fail weeks later at the worst moment.

## Mistakes I made

- **Too much solder** — bridges everything. Use less than you think; you can always add.
- **Moving while cooling** — creates the dreaded cold joint. Hold still for one full second.
- **The wrong tip** — a worn, blackened tip won't transfer heat and you end up cooking the pad instead of soldering it.

## When to stop

If a pad lifts off the board, stop, step away, and either patch with a bodge wire or redesign. Desoldering braid fixes most mistakes early, but a lifted pad is the point where persistence costs more than a new board.
